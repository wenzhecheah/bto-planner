How BTO Works

Your first home, from application to keys.

How BTO works Planner Data & sources

A BTO (Build-To-Order) flat is a new, subsidised HDB flat that you apply for before it is built. HDB launches projects a few times a year. A computer ballot decides the order in which applicants choose a flat, and the flat is usually ready about years later.

The journey, step by step

  1. Get your HDB Flat Eligibility (HFE) letter

    At least a month before the launch

    Apply on the HDB Flat Portal. The letter tells you upfront whether you can buy, and how much grant and HDB loan you can get. You need a valid one to apply. If your income, job or household changes, you must cancel it and apply again.

    You pay: nothing.

  2. Apply in the launch

    -day window

    Choose one flat type in one town. It is a ballot, not first-come-first-served, so there is no need to rush. Watch the application rates during the window: a rate of about 1 or lower means good odds.

    You pay: application fee.

  3. Ballot result

    Within months

    You get a queue number. If it is within the number of flats, HDB invites you to choose one. Turning the chance down when flats are still available has a penalty (see below).

    You pay: nothing.

  4. Book your flat

    When your queue number comes up

    Pick your unit at your booking appointment, which you must attend. This is also the only time to opt for floor finishes, doors and sanitary fittings (Optional Component Scheme). With a bank loan, get the bank's Letter of Offer after booking.

    You pay: the option fee, in cash: .

  5. Sign the Agreement for Lease

    Within months of booking

    The legal agreement to buy the flat. Your grant is paid into your CPF Ordinary Account around now.

    You pay: the first part of the downpayment, stamp duty and the legal fee, mostly from CPF OA (table below).

  6. Wait for the flat to be built

    About years ( for Shorter Waiting Time projects)

    Track the Probable Completion Date on MyHDB. Plan where you'll live until then, with some slack for delays. HDB checks your finances again before paying out the loan, so avoid new loans and keep your job steady.

    You pay: nothing to HDB. Keep saving for renovation.

  7. Collect your keys

    When the flat is ready (within month of HDB's notice)

    The loan is paid out and you become the owner. With an HDB loan you need HDB fire insurance, and the Home Protection Scheme if CPF pays your instalments. Instalments start on the 1st of the second month after the loan is paid out.

    You pay: the rest of the downpayment, small fees, and then your renovation (cash).

  8. Live in it: the minimum occupation period (MOP)

    Move in within months; MOP of years (Standard) or years (Plus, Prime)

    Everyone listed must live there. Until the MOP ends you cannot sell, rent out the whole flat, or buy private property. Engaged couples must marry within months of completing the purchase.

    You pay: monthly instalments, service and conservancy charges, property tax.

Can you buy?

  • At least one of you is a Singapore Citizen, and the other a Citizen or PR. A Citizen + PR household pays a premium, refunded when the PR becomes a Citizen or you have a Citizen child.
  • You are at least , married or engaged (Fiancé/Fiancée Scheme).
  • Your household earns up to a month (from ; with extended family). HDB uses the average over the 12 months ending 2 months before the HFE application.
  • Neither of you owns a private home, local or overseas, or sold one in the last months.

Standard, Plus and Prime

Every project is classified by location. Plus and Prime flats are in better locations and get more subsidy, so they come with conditions. Check these before you apply: they matter if you might sell or rent out later.

Grants

First-timer couples can get the Enhanced CPF Housing Grant (EHG) of up to , paid into your CPF Ordinary Accounts. The lower your income, the bigger the grant; above a month there is none. One of you must have worked for 12 months in a row. If one of you has had a housing subsidy before, the grant is up to , based on half your income.

Paying for your flat

You pay about a quarter of the price yourselves, in two parts: at the Agreement for Lease and at key collection. Most couples pay it from CPF OA (including the grant). The loan covers the rest. If the younger of you applies for the HFE letter by your th birthday, less is due at the first payment.

HDB loan or bank loan?

You can switch from an HDB loan to a bank loan later, but not back.

Your ballot chances

  • First-timer couples who are married and 40 or younger, or have or expect a Singapore Citizen child, get chances. Other first-timer couples get ; second-timers get 1.
  • After 2 or more unsuccessful applications for Standard flats, you get an extra chance for each further Standard application.
  • From the launch, first-timer families get 1 more chance for each Singapore Citizen child aged 18 or below.
  • Most flats are set aside for first-timers. Applying where the first-timer application rate is low beats applying where it is high.

If you change your mind

Things couples often miss

  • Renovation is cash. CPF can't pay for it, and it comes right after key collection.
  • A pay rise can shrink your grant. The grant uses past income, in steps of $500.00.
  • The loan is checked again before keys. A job change or new car loan in the years of waiting can reduce it.
  • Bank loans need cash upfront: at least 5% of the price, at the Agreement for Lease.
  • CPF used for your home is paid back into CPF, with interest, when you sell. Keep some OA as a buffer if you can.
  • Plus and Prime flats pay part of the resale price back to HDB, and can never be rented out whole.

Words you'll see

HFE letter
HDB Flat Eligibility letter: confirms you can buy, and your grant and HDB loan amounts.
AFL
Agreement for Lease: the agreement to buy, signed months after booking. The first downpayment is due then.
EHG
Enhanced CPF Housing Grant, for first-timers, based on income.
OA
CPF Ordinary Account: the part of your CPF you can use for housing.
LTV
Loan-to-value: the most you can borrow, as a share of the price ().
MSR
Mortgage servicing ratio: your instalment can be at most of your gross monthly income.
TDSR
Total debt servicing ratio (bank loans): all your loan repayments can be at most of income.
MOP
Minimum occupation period: years you must live in the flat before selling or renting it out whole.
FT(PMC)
First-Timer (Parents & Married Couples): the group with the most ballot chances.
